  • OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 “Sol” on Thursday after a US government hold. The Department of Commerce’s Center for AI Standards and Innovation cleared the public launch after additional safety tests; OpenAI had openly criticised the delay as keeping its best tools from developers. The flagship “Sol Ultra” variant tops TerminalBench 2.1 at 91.9 % vs Claude Mythos 5 (88.0 %) and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (70.7 %), and Sol matches Mythos on cyber tasks at roughly a third of the tokens, while costing $5/$30 per MTok — half of Anthropic’s Fable 5. Source: the-decoder.com · Axios via the-decoder

  • MiniMax plans a 2.7-trillion-parameter open-source model — internally “M3 Pro” — for Q3 2026. Per The Information, the release would be the largest open-weight model from a Chinese lab, more than 6× the size of MiniMax’s current 428B-parameter M3. The move signals that the Chinese open-source push (Zhipu, DeepSeek, Moonshot) is now competing on absolute scale, but it lands as Beijing weighs tighter export-style controls on domestic frontier releases. Source: the-decoder.com

  • Meta’s Superintelligence Labs launches Muse Image, the first release under Alexandr Wang. Unlike a prompt-to-pixel generator, Muse works agentically — it writes and runs code, calls web search, and self-revises before producing images. It debuts #2 on Image Arena behind OpenAI’s GPT Image 2 and ahead of Nano Banana and Grok Imagine, but the training data (Instagram photos) is already drawing scrutiny. Source: ai.meta.com · the-decoder.com

  • SambaNova raises $1 B Series F first close at an $11 B valuation, just 5 months after its $350 M Series E. The round was led by General Atlantic with Intel re-upping, deepening their Xeon-based AI-inference partnership; the chipmaker walked away from acquiring SambaNova at ~$1.6 B in December. CEO Rodrigo Liang left an IPO path open and said a second close is “a few weeks” away — a notable signal of confidence in non-NVIDIA inference silicon. Source: TechCrunch

  • “GitLost” — Noma Labs shows GitHub’s new Agentic Workflows can be tricked into leaking private repos. A crafted public GitHub Issue in any organisation repo can inject instructions that steer the Claude/Copilot-backed agent into exfiltrating data from sibling private repositories. The disclosure lands weeks after GitHub launched the markdown-style workflow feature and is the first high-profile indirect prompt-injection bug against a major platform’s agent surface. Source: noma.security

  • Qwen-AgentWorld (arXiv 2606.24597) — a model trained to be the environment, beats Opus 4.8 on world-prediction. The Alibaba/Qwen 397B variant scores 58.71 on Qwen’s own env-prediction benchmark, ahead of GPT-5.4 (58.25), Claude Opus 4.8 (56.59) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (54.57). The interesting claim is not the leaderboard but the framing: agents can now train inside a fully controllable text-based simulator, and learning to predict world state appears to also make the model a better agent. Source: Towards AI · arXiv:2606.24597

  • MGX (Abu Dhabi) closes a $49 B AI-focused fund, beating its $45 B target. The vehicle has already invested across 14 companies spanning semis, infrastructure and platforms, and is developing a 3 GW AI campus near Paris while co-owning Aligned Data Centres via a $40 B consortium. The close hardens the Gulf as a structural backer of US/EU AI compute and is the largest AI-dedicated fund announced in 2026. Source: Crescendo.ai tracker · The National

  • Anthropic’s Claude Sonnet 5 ships alongside a self-hosted Claude Code gateway on AWS Bedrock and GCP Vertex. Sonnet 5 targets lower-cost multi-step agent work (also GA on GitHub Copilot across VS Code, JetBrains, Xcode, Eclipse), and the BYOC gateway keeps code, credentials and audit logs inside the customer’s own cloud. Discounted API rates run through 31 August; the structural play is to undercut Codex and Gemini Code Assist for regulated enterprises. Source: winbuzzer.com · fourweekmba.com · GitHub Changelog
